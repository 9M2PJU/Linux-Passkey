The first release of Linux Passkey: Windows Hello-style passkeys for Linux. Your computer becomes a built-in passkey authenticator, and you confirm each passkey with your Linux password or fingerprint. No phone or hardware key is needed.

## Highlights

- **A built-in authenticator in every major browser.** With the browser extension, Chrome, Chromium, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi and Firefox see a user-verifying platform authenticator, as on Windows and macOS, so sites stop warning about partial passkey support. Requests meant for a hardware key or a phone still go to the browser's own dialog.
- **Also a USB security key.** Browsers without the extension see the computer as a plugged-in FIDO2 key, through the kernel's `uhid` module.
- **CTAP 2.1:** passkeys and second-factor credentials, ES256 and EdDSA keys, `credProtect`, `hmac-secret` (WebAuthn PRF), credential management and account selection.
- **Your system login verifies you.** The check goes through polkit, which accepts your password, or your fingerprint if fingerprint login is on. It is never cached.
- **Encrypted at rest.** Private keys are wrapped with AES-256-GCM under a master key in your login keyring.
- **The Passkeys app** lets you search, inspect and delete passkeys, see the service status, and reset everything.
- **Works on any desktop.** The GNOME Shell extension (GNOME 45–49) is optional: it shows prompts as system dialogs and adds a top-bar menu. Without it, prompts open in a window of their own on KDE, Xfce and other desktops.

## Install

Signed repositories for Debian/Ubuntu, Fedora/RHEL 10, openSUSE Tumbleweed and Arch are at **[packages.akileswar.com/linux-passkey](https://packages.akileswar.com/linux-passkey/)**. The [README](https://github.com/HaythmKenway/Linux-Passkey#install) has the commands for each distribution. Leave out `gnome-shell-extension-linux-passkey` if you don't use GNOME. Log out and back in after installing.

The browser extension is not on the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons yet; load it as described in the [README](https://github.com/HaythmKenway/Linux-Passkey#the-browser-extension).

## Known limitations

- Passkeys are device-bound and not synced. Back up your login keyring and `~/.local/share/linux-passkey` together, because neither works without the other.
- Passkey autofill (conditional mediation) still goes to the browser; use the site's *Sign in with a passkey* button.
- Snap-packaged browsers may not see the USB key, because snapd's allow-list may not include its test USB ID. Use the browser's `.deb` or Flatpak build, or the browser extension.
- The GNOME extension supports GNOME 45–49. GNOME 50 (Ubuntu 26.04) is not tested yet.
- Kernels without `uhid` (minimal and server installs) need `linux-modules-extra-$(uname -r)` on Debian and Ubuntu.
- No U2F/CTAP1 and no client PIN. Verification is always your system login.

See the [README](https://github.com/HaythmKenway/Linux-Passkey#readme) for how it works, security notes and troubleshooting.
